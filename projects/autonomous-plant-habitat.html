Autonomous Plant Habitat

An evolving engineering project exploring how plants can be grown inside a controlled environment through monitoring, automation, mechanical design, and repeatable experiments.

Plant growth Environmental control Automation Build log
Current status: This project is in its initial concept and research stage. The design, hardware, control platform, and experimental setup will be selected progressively as the project develops.

Project vision

The long-term goal is to create a small habitat capable of maintaining suitable conditions for plant growth with limited manual intervention.

The system will be developed through a series of small experiments rather than as one large final build. Each stage will introduce a new capability, generate data, and help guide the next design decision.

The project will also serve as a platform for learning about plant biology, environmental sensing, control, automation, mechanical design, and experimental engineering.

Initial questions

The first phase of the project will focus on understanding the environmental conditions that most strongly influence plant growth.

  • Which environmental variables should be monitored?
  • How frequently should measurements be collected?
  • How can watering be controlled reliably?
  • How should light, airflow, and humidity be managed?
  • Which plants are most suitable for the first experiments?
  • How can growth and plant health be measured objectively?
  • How can the habitat be made modular and easy to modify?

Each stage will be evaluated before additional complexity is introduced.

Planned development stages

1. Plant and environment research

Select suitable plants and study their requirements for water, light, temperature, humidity, airflow, soil, and available growing space.

2. Habitat design

Develop the physical enclosure, plant supports, water storage, drainage, lighting arrangement, and access for maintenance.

3. Environmental monitoring

Add measurements progressively and evaluate their reliability before using them for automated decisions.

4. Data collection

Store environmental measurements and observations so that plant growth can be compared across time and between experiments.

5. Automated watering

Develop a repeatable watering system and compare scheduled watering with condition-based control.

6. Environmental control

Investigate how lighting, airflow, temperature, and humidity can be adjusted within the habitat.

7. Controlled experiments

Test how individual environmental variables affect plant growth, health, water use, and development.

8. Adaptive operation

Use collected data to improve control rules and eventually explore adaptive or predictive strategies.

Experimental strategy

The project will use small, controlled experiments in which one or a limited number of variables are changed at a time.

Possible experiments include:

  • Comparing different watering strategies
  • Measuring soil moisture before and after watering
  • Comparing natural and artificial lighting
  • Studying the effect of airflow
  • Comparing growth under different humidity conditions
  • Testing different soils or growing media
  • Measuring water consumption over time
  • Tracking plant height, leaf development, and growth rate

Long-term direction

Once the basic habitat is reliable, the project could be extended to reproduce environmental conditions that differ from normal indoor cultivation.

Possible future directions include:

  • Closed or partially closed water cycles
  • Reduced-resource cultivation
  • Remote monitoring
  • Multiple plant zones
  • Automated experiment scheduling
  • Computer-vision-based growth monitoring
  • Predictive environmental control
  • Simplified analog environments inspired by lunar or Martian habitats

These are future possibilities rather than requirements for the first prototype.

Build log

August 2026

Project definition

Defined the initial idea for a modular habitat that can support progressively more advanced plant-growth experiments.

The current focus is researching plant requirements, defining the first experiment, and determining the minimum viable habitat needed to begin collecting useful observations.

Next steps

  1. Select the first plant species.
  2. Define the environmental variables for the first experiment.
  3. Sketch the initial habitat and watering concept.
  4. Establish a simple method for recording plant growth.
  5. Build the first non-automated prototype.
  6. Collect baseline measurements before adding automation.

What this project will demonstrate

The project is intended to demonstrate iterative system development, experimental design, environmental monitoring, automation, data collection, mechanical prototyping, control, and the integration of biological and engineering requirements.
